Tail of the Dragon, Beartooth Highway and Blue Ridge Parkway by motorcycle

Three American roads come up again and again when riders talk about the US: the Tail of the Dragon on US 129, the Beartooth Highway between Montana and Wyoming, and the Blue Ridge Parkway along the Appalachians. They could hardly be more different. One is a short, tight run of bends, one a high mountain road open only in summer, one a slow parkway of several hundred miles. This guide collects what the road agencies, the National Park Service and the Forest Service publish about each, and shows how to put them into a tour you draw yourself.

The Tail of the Dragon is a day’s highlight, not a day’s ride. The Beartooth Highway takes the Forest Service’s minimum estimate of two to three hours one way. The Blue Ridge Parkway needs several days end to end: the Park Service reckons 30 miles of parkway at about an hour.

Tail of the Dragon (US 129, Deals Gap)

The Dragon is the stretch of US 129 that crosses the state line between North Carolina and Tennessee at Deals Gap, 1,988 ft (606 m) high. Wikipedia describes the Tennessee part as an 11-mile (18 km) stretch that is said to have 318 curves; that number is a local claim, not a surveyed count. In North Carolina the road is called Tapoco Road, in Tennessee Calderwood Highway, and it is signed US 129 throughout.

Part of the road forms the southwestern border of Great Smoky Mountains National Park, and there is no development along the 11 miles. The Deals Gap community sits at the junction with NC 28, about 0.7 mi (1.1 km) south of the gap.

Speed limit and enforcement

The limit was 55 mph before 1992 and has been 30 mph (48 km/h) since 2005. According to Wikipedia, police presence on the Tennessee side has increased sharply since 2007, and the Blount County Rescue Squad stands by on summer weekends. A weekday morning usually means fewer cars and fewer riders.

Deals Gap has its own pass page with the gaps nearby, Newfound Gap in the Smokies among them, and the mountain passes in Tennessee list shows the rest of the state.

Beartooth Highway (US 212)

The Beartooth Highway carries US 212 from Red Lodge, Montana, over Beartooth Pass in Wyoming and back into Montana at Cooke City, to the Northeast Entrance of Yellowstone National Park. Wikipedia gives the length as 68.3 miles (110 km). The Custer Gallatin National Forest puts Beartooth Pass at 10,947 ft (3,337 m) and calls the road an All-American Road. From Red Lodge it climbs in switchbacks up to the Montana–Wyoming state line.

Near Beartooth Lake the road meets Wyoming Highway 296, the Chief Joseph byway, a second way in or out if you do not want to ride the same road back.

Season

The Montana Department of Transportation says the highway is generally open from the Friday of Memorial Day weekend through mid-October, weather permitting, and that unpredictable weather can close it for a while even in summer. The Forest Service gives Memorial Day to 15 October and notes that snow often lies at the higher elevations until early or mid-July. Look at the MDT road page or the 511 map on the day you ride.

MDT maintains the road up to the Montana–Wyoming border; from there the National Park Service is responsible through Wyoming and back into Montana. Construction began in 1932, and the road opened on 14 June 1936. See the Beartooth Pass page and the mountain passes in Wyoming for the passes around it.

Blue Ridge Parkway

The Blue Ridge Parkway is a 469-mile (755 km) road run by the National Park Service that links Shenandoah National Park in Virginia with Great Smoky Mountains National Park in North Carolina. Mileposts start at 0 at Rockfish Gap in Virginia and reach 469 at US 441 near Cherokee. The highest point is Richland Balsam at milepost 431, 6,053 ft (1,845 m).

What the Park Service tells riders

The Park Service reports up to 250 accidents involving motorcycles on the Parkway each year, more than half of them with serious injuries. Its Rule the Ridge campaign has three messages: ride single file, ride in lane position 2 and keep your eyes on the road. Shoulders are narrow in places, some curves tighten halfway through, and oncoming cars can cut across the centre line in blind bends.

Winter

The Parkway is not maintained in winter, and sections at high elevation and through the tunnels are often closed from late fall to early spring. The Park Service adds that closures can come suddenly; look at its road status page before you set off.

Planning a ride over them

  • Top up before you start a section: there is no fuel along the Dragon’s 11 miles or on the Blue Ridge Parkway itself.
  • Split the Parkway into day stages at access points where you sleep and refuel.
  • On the Beartooth, check the season and the day’s weather; a sunny morning in Red Lodge does not mean a clear pass.
  • Leave room in the day for the slow speed limits, especially on the Parkway.

For an overview of how to set up stops and stages, see how to plan a motorcycle trip. If you prefer side roads on the way to the famous ones, avoiding motorways on a motorcycle route shows the approach.

Draw your own American road trip

Pick your start and end, then add the roads as stops: Deals Gap for the Dragon, Red Lodge and Cooke City for the Beartooth, and the access points where each Parkway stage begins and ends. Drag the stops into the order you will ride them. A long press on the map adds a place without a name, such as an overlook or a fuel stop just off the Parkway. Frequently asked questions

  • How long is the Tail of the Dragon?

    The Tennessee stretch of US 129 at Deals Gap is 11 miles (18 km). It is said to have 318 curves; that number is a local claim, not a surveyed count.

  • What is the speed limit on the Tail of the Dragon?

    30 mph (48 km/h) since 2005. Police presence on the Tennessee side has increased sharply since 2007.

  • When is the Beartooth Highway open?

    The Montana Department of Transportation says it is generally open from the Friday of Memorial Day weekend through mid-October, weather permitting. Weather can close it for a while even in summer.

  • How high is the Beartooth Pass?

    10,947 ft (3,337 m), according to the Custer Gallatin National Forest.

  • Can you get fuel on the Blue Ridge Parkway?

    No. The National Park Service says there are no gas stations along the Parkway, but several are just off it at access points.

  • What is the speed limit on the Blue Ridge Parkway?

    45 mph at most, and in places 35 or 25 mph. The Park Service reckons about an hour for 30 miles.
